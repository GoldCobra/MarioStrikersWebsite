The Mario Strikers Charged (Football) Competitive Ruleset

Last revised April 16, 2026

1. Introduction

These rules establish a Competition Committee and define a standardized competitive ruleset.

2. Gameplay Settings

2.1 Gameplay

2.1.1 Game Version

The official tournament version is the PAL version (RQ4P01) on Nintendo Wii or Nintendo Wii U.

This is because most players in the current community use the PAL version. Matchmaking between PAL and NTSC is technically impossible due to region lock.

Both console and Dolphin Emulator are permitted. Players must be able to play via Wiimmfi. Netplay is not feasible due to technical limitations.

As of Dolphin version 2603a or newer, Dolphin natively supports Wiimmfi matchmaking for matches between Dolphin Emulator and console (Wii/Wii U), without additional mods or Gecko codes.

Players using the emulator must use Dolphin 2603a or newer for official cross-platform play.

2.1.2 Match Settings

  • Skill Level: ANY
  • Game Type: 10 GOALS
  • Environment Cheats: SECURE STADIA
  • Power Up Cheats: NONE
  • Player Cheats: NONE

2.1.3 Stadiums

The default stadium is BOWSER STADIUM. If players mutually agree, they may pick any stadium except THE SAND TOMB or THUNDER ISLAND.

From the moment a player clicks the "accept" button of an online invitation, they agree to play in the selected stadium for that game. Selecting the wrong stadium is not a valid reason to restart the game unless the selected stadium is illegal.

If players are not using the fast terrain Gecko code, selection is restricted to the following four stadiums: BOWSER STADIUM, THE CLASSROOM, THE LAVA PIT, CRYSTAL CANYON.

2.1.4 Match Procedure

The following steps define the match procedure and are intended to ensure that both players can play on Home over the course of the match.

  1. Players decide who will play on Home and Away in the first game. The criteria for side selection are defined in Section 2.1.5. The Away player decides whether to pick captain first or second.
  2. Players play the first game of the set.
  3. The losing player of the preceding game picks either Home or Away for the next game. The Away player decides whether to pick captain first or second.
  4. The next game is played.
  5. Repeat steps 2 through 4 for all subsequent games until the set is completed.
  6. When a set is completed, repeat the same process for the next set, starting from Step 1.
  7. The match ends when a player reaches the required number of set wins. In most cases, one set wins the match, but this may vary by tournament rules.

2.1.5 First Game Side Selection

The following rules determine which player chooses side in Game 1. If two or more rules conflict, the first applicable rule in this list has priority.

  1. If the match is a best-of-X sets, the player who lost in the previous set picks side during Game 1 of the upcoming set. This does not apply to Set 1.
  2. If a tournament has multiple stages, the first stage determines seeding. In later stages, the higher-seeded player picks side.
  3. If the tournament is the MSL World Championship, the player with the most MSL points gets side pick.
  4. If none of the above apply, players flip a coin (for example in our Discord), and the winner gets side pick.

2.2 Prohibited Techniques and Selections

2.2.1 NK-Bug

VIDEO

NK-Bug in any form is prohibited due to its game-breaking behavior. It will result in disqualification from the whole tournament, along with the next seasonal tournament.

If a player unintentionally scores an NK-Bug goal while more than one goal away from victory, they must score an own goal. After that, the match continues normally.

If a player unintentionally scores an NK-Bug goal as the final goal of the match, that goal does not count and the match must be replayed from the score before the NK-Bug goal.

2.2.2 Insta Chip

VIDEO

Insta Chip or Midcourt Chip after kickoff is prohibited due to its non-competitive nature and results in an automatic loss of the game. To prevent an Insta Chip, the player with kickoff must lose contact with the ball at least once before executing a Midcourt Chip.

* The "player with kickoff" is the player who starts with ball possession, not the opponent. If a player starts without ball possession at kickoff (for example after scoring, or at 0-0 kickoff), these restrictions do not apply to that player.

** A player loses contact with the ball when any of the following happens: pass, shot, Z-chip, goalkeeper steal, or knockdown resulting in loss of control.

*** For an Insta Chip to count as such, it must be a Midcourt Chip. The following do not count as Midcourt Chip examples: WALL-LUIGI boosted chips, Shroom Chips, and the Bowser Shuttle.

2.2.3 Boo Deke Goals

VIDEO

Boo deking through the opposing goalkeeper is prohibited due to its non-competitive nature and possible activation of NK-Bug, and it results in an automatic loss of the game.

2.2.4 Hitspam

VIDEO

Excessive hitspam is prohibited due to its non-competitive nature and results in an automatic loss of the game.

2.2.5 Megastrike

Mega Strikes are prohibited due to their non-competitive nature. If a Megastrike is executed accidentally, any goal scored through it does not count and the match must be replayed from the score before the Megastrike.

2.2.6 Cheat Codes

Cross-platform play is officially supported natively through Wiimmfi when using Dolphin 2603a or newer.

Gecko codes that create unfair advantages or disrupt gameplay (such as lag generators) are strictly forbidden.

2.3 Match Disruptions

2.3.1 Disconnections

Disconnecting during a game (rage quit, etc.) results in an automatic loss of the game. However, there is no reliable way to prove whether a disconnect was intentional or caused by connection issues. If the issue occurred unintentionally, restart the game and score goals until the score matches the state before disconnection.

Once scores match the previous state, the player who had ball possession before disconnecting must pass the ball to their goalie, and the game restarts from there.

2.3.2 Restarts

Tournament organizers may approve a game restart in exceptional circumstances, such as a power outage. Section 2.3.1 may apply in this case.

2.3.3 Stalling

Stalling or excessively delaying the game or match may result in a game or match forfeit at the discretion of tournament organizers.

2.3.4 Game Disruptions

If a game is played with misconfigured settings (as described in Sections 2.1.2 and 2.1.3) that could materially affect the result, a player may petition tournament organizers for a replay. The final decision is at organizer discretion. This request must be made immediately after that game; once the next game starts, the previous game cannot be replayed.

2.3.5 Penalties

Players who break the rules in this document are subject to penalties, including but not limited to:

  • Game restart
  • Loss of a game
  • Loss of a match
  • Tournament forfeiture

Any penalties imposed on a player may be made public by tournament organizers at their sole discretion.

3. Code of Conduct

All players are expected to exhibit good sportsmanship and maintain respect for one another and for all tournament organizers. Players are expected to play at their best at all times and avoid behavior inconsistent with good sportsmanship, honesty, and fair play. Behavior considered inappropriate includes, but is not limited to:

  • Interfering with tournament operation
  • Acting in an unsportsmanlike or disruptive manner, or annoying, abusing, threatening, or harassing others
  • Engaging in collusion (for example any agreement to predetermine outcomes)
  • Cheating by any means
  • Intentionally delaying or tampering with gameplay
  • Offensive, vulgar, or obscene gamertags
  • Sexism, ageism, racism, or any other form of prejudice or bigotry
  • Engaging in violence or behavior deemed immoral, unethical, disgraceful, or indecent by tournament owners
  • Offering gifts or rewards to gain competitive advantage or create competitive disadvantage for opponents
  • Otherwise violating these rules
